# What `f_poincare` taught us for `f_apollonian` and `f_conformal_fill`

_Written 2026-11-07, at Matt's request, at the end of the `f_poincare` build. Sources are the Record in `.specify/stable/f_poincare/tasks.md` (task ids in brackets), the codebox `src/f_poincare/codebox_poincare.gen` and the mirror `tests/modules/poincare/poincare_mirror.py`. **Observed** = measured offline by Claude in float64 or float32, or on the GPU bench / live by Matt, as the tasks Record says; **inferred** = worked out and not run. `f_apollonian` is paused (gate `P-moebius-tiling`): this note does not resume it, it records what a future resume or spec could reuse._

## For `f_apollonian` (paused, ADR-8: the parity-tracking bug)

1. **Build a float64 mirror before the codebox, and test it against closed forms the mirror does not use.** Poincare's geometry was checked by two independent hyperbolic-trigonometry derivations, the camera by isometry (hyperbolic distance preserved), the folds by "settled points lie outside every edge circle" [T002]. Apollonian's bug lived only in Max: `use_mapped=4` (zero error) and `debug_ok` (pass) never agreed and the session ended without reconciling them (`.specify/paused/f_apollonian/plan.md` ADR-8 record). Recommendation (not tested): reproduce the accumulated-transform loop in NumPy first and compare the two views offline, where a mismatch can be isolated.
2. **The mirror caught a hidden dependency: the order of the circles.** When a point lies inside more than one edge circle, the settled position depends on which circle wins; lowest-index-wins and highest-index-wins disagree on a large share of points. The cause is inferred (at {4,5} the tile is not a strict fundamental domain) [T002]. Apollonian's ring-plus-centre construction also has overlapping candidates, so check order-dependence in the mirror before trusting any orientation or parity claim.
3. **Branchless settle works on the GPU; an early exit is not needed.** `f_poincare` runs a fixed 32 iterations with each step multiplied by a `has` flag (a no-op once settled) [T010, T011]. This answers the fallback in `ideas/f_apollonian.md` (the `break` question, still untested) with a working pattern. Also verified on the GPU: a multi-return call inside a `for` loop, and a user function that calls another (compiled first time) [T011, T022].
4. **Test only the three circles nearest by angle, not all N.** Index the circle by `atan2` sector and test it plus its two neighbours, lowest index wins. Observed to equal the full p-circle chain on every pixel both settle at all 56 pairs p, q in 3..10; one nearest (19.7% wrong at {4,5}) and two nearest (fails at q = 3) were rejected; nearest by hyperbolic distance was worse [T005]. The same trick should cut the cost of Apollonian's N-circle ring (inferred: its overlap count is not measured).
5. **Tangent points are knife-edges: give "settled" a tolerance.** Pixels on a mirror line of the group land exactly on an edge circle; float64 called them unsettled and float32 settled. `SETTLE_TOL` 1e-5 (squared distance) fixed it in both modes [T020]. Apollonian's "rainbow swirl at tangent points" is plausibly the same class of effect (inferred).
6. **The unsettled boundary has a measured policy.** Measure the unsettled fraction at 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 iterations and the radius where it starts, then fade unsettled pixels to black from just inside it [T004]: <= 0.04% unsettled at 32 iterations, fade from 0.99. Apollonian's non-converged fractal boundary is the same question ("what do unsettled pixels show"); use the same measurement before choosing a count.
7. **Float32 drift grows with a camera or final inversion that pushes mass toward the rim.** With the translation at |a| = 0.99 colour errors jump from about 0.1% of pixels at r 0.9-0.95 to 6-30% at r 0.95-0.98 for {3,7}; the fade start moves in by 0.04 (|a| / 0.99)^2 [T032]. Apollonian's one animated final inversion (`ideas/f_apollonian.md`) should be checked the same way.
8. **Per-region texture sampling may not need the mapped-circle machinery.** `f_poincare` samples at the settled position of the fold; that position is already a scale-free local frame for the region the pixel ended in, and it needed no accumulated matrix (the scratch patch's `N` matrices and `debug_ok` were dropped [T001, spec]). Apollonian's flat-disc colouring does need a true containment test (ADR-8); its texture sampling (production item 2) might not. Inferred, untested.
9. **Practical rules re-learned:** name Params so they do not collide with `jit.gl.pix` attributes (`fold_mode`, not `mode`) and never one letter (`p_sides`, `q_vertex`); one codebox for two looks stays well under the parser budget (about 85 statements against 250 / 450) [T021]; a ramp test texture (R = u, G = v, B = u v) makes the sampled colour BE the settled position, so one bench comparison checks position and colour (`tests/modules/poincare/bench_poincare.py`).

## For `f_conformal_fill` (captured, not specced)

1. **The "thin consumer" half already exists, almost.** `f_poincare` is camera, then fold, then sample. Its start is `z0 = norm` rescaled; for a conformal fill, `z0` is instead read from the precomputed field (a float32 texture, complex value in R and G, shape mask in a spare channel). Everything after that line is reusable as it stands (inferred: not built or benched with a field input). The offline solver is then the only novel part.
2. **The camera answers two open questions.** (a) Pole choice: a conformal map of a simply connected shape onto the disk is unique up to the three-parameter family of disk automorphisms (a rotation and a translation), which is exactly `rot`, `cam_x`, `cam_y`. So the two boundary points the paper fixes need not be a user decision; any choice gives the same tiling up to a camera move (theory, not tested). (b) Live animation: the solve stays static per shape, but flying through the tessellation inside the shape costs nothing extra, because only the post-lookup isometry changes. Observed on `f_poincare`: smooth at 60 fps live, and the zero camera is bit-exact [T030, T033].
3. **The shape's edge is the disk's rim, with all its problems.** Pixels near the boundary have unbounded fold depth and float32 error is worst there [T004, T032]. Policy to reuse: unsettled pixels fade to black, a fade from about 0.99 of the disk radius (earlier under a camera). Consequence worth checking: how many pixels near the shape's outline that fade covers depends on the local conformal scaling, so the inset could be uneven (inferred). A float32 field texture is needed, and a low-resolution solve upsampled bilinearly looks safe in the interior but the boundary band is where interpolation error would be amplified (inferred, matters for the paper's resolution question).
4. **Test the consumer without the solver.** Feed the codebox a synthetic field (the identity, or an analytic Möbius map, written as a texture) and compare with the float64 mirror, exactly as `bench_poincare.py` does. Test the solver separately with invariants that do not use its own formula: the discrete harmonic residual, angle preservation, and "a disk-shaped input maps to a disk automorphism". That keeps the two builds independently verifiable, which is how `f_poincare` was kept honest.
5. **Texture mapping choices carry over:** crop, not warp, with `tile_scale` setting the texture extent [T006]; Whole and Sliver both take live p and q, and the validity guard (`1/p + 1/q < 1/2`, q raised to the minimum) is reusable as written.

## Not learned (so nobody assumes it was)

Nothing here tests half-plane or other models, a non-disk generating set, N-matrix tracking, the Apollonian packing itself, or any solve. `f_poincare` is closed-form per pixel; the harmonic solve is untouched.
